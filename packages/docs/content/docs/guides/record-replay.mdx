---
title: Record a real charger and replay it
description: Put the proxy between a charger and your CSMS, record the session, replay it against another CSMS, and turn it into a scenario.
---

A recording of a real charger is the most direct regression test for a CSMS: it holds exactly what that charger sends, in the order and at the pace it sends it. wattsim records through a proxy, replays a recording against any CSMS and compares the answers, and can turn a recording into an editable scenario.

## 1. Record

Start the proxy in front of your CSMS:

```sh
wattsim proxy --listen 9000 --upstream ws://csms.example/ocpp --record session.jsonl
```

Then change the charger's CSMS URL to point at the proxy, `ws://<proxy-host>:9000/<anything>/<identity>`. For each charger that connects, the proxy opens `<upstream>/<identity>`, offers the charger's subprotocols and passes its `Authorization` header on, then completes the charger's handshake with the subprotocol your CSMS chose. If your CSMS refuses the connection, the charger gets the same HTTP status.

Frames pass through byte for byte, including ones that do not parse, and nothing is queued or reordered. A close on one side closes the other with the same code. Every frame is appended to `session.jsonl` as it passes.

`--listen` binds to `127.0.0.1` by default. A charger on the network needs `--listen 0.0.0.0:9000`.

Leave it running long enough to cover what you want to test: a boot, a few transactions, a remote start, whatever your CSMS needs to get right.

<Callout type="warn">
A recording holds identities, id tags and, in the `Authorization` header passed through, possibly credentials. Treat trace files as secrets: do not commit them to a public repository.
</Callout>

## 2. Replay against another CSMS

```sh
wattsim replay session.jsonl --csms ws://staging.example/ocpp --virtual
```

Replay connects once per charger identity in the trace and sends the charger's frames again. Every frame the CSMS sent in the recording becomes an expectation:

- A CSMS CALL in the trace matches the next CALL of the same action from the CSMS under test.
- A CSMS answer matches the answer to the charger CALL it answered.
- Payloads are compared like `expect` in a scenario: every key in the recording must match, extra keys are fine. Timestamps and ids the CSMS assigns (`transactionId`, `requestId`, `remoteStartId`, `chargingProfileId` and the like) are removed before comparing. `evseId` and `connectorId` are always compared.

Mismatches print as `session.jsonl:<line>: <identity>: <what differed>`. A CSMS frame the recording does not have is a mismatch too. Exit code 0 means everything matched, 1 means at least one mismatch (a refused connection included), 2 means the trace could not be read or a flag was wrong. That makes a replay a CI check: record once against the CSMS version you trust, replay against every new build.

### Pacing

Without `--virtual`, frames go out at the recorded pace; `--speed 10` replays ten times faster. With `--virtual`, timing is ignored and each charger frame goes out as soon as the CSMS has answered everything before it. Use `--virtual` for functional regressions and the recorded pace when your CSMS's timers matter.

### Different identities and credentials

```sh
wattsim replay session.jsonl --csms wss://staging.example/ocpp \
  --identity CP-REAL-01=CP-TEST-01 --auth CP-TEST-01:test-password
```

`--identity NEW` renames the only charger of a one-charger trace; `OLD=NEW[,OLD=NEW]` renames several. `--auth user:password` sends HTTP Basic credentials in the handshake. `--timeout` sets how many seconds to wait for each expected CSMS frame (default 30), and `--settle` how long to keep listening after the last entry so a late unexpected frame is still caught (default 1).

## 3. Inject faults while recording

The proxy takes the same chaos rules as a scenario, without the `chaos` keyword. `out` is charger to CSMS, `in` is CSMS to charger:

```sh
wattsim proxy --listen 9000 --upstream ws://csms.example/ocpp --record lossy.jsonl \
  --chaos "drop out MeterValues nth 3; delay 2s in every 2"
```

This puts a real charger behind a bad network without touching its firmware. The trace records each frame as its sender sent it, with the rule that touched it noted, and replay sends what actually reached the CSMS: dropped frames are skipped, changed ones go out as delivered. `--seed` fixes the outcome of `probability` rules.

## 4. Turn the recording into a scenario

```sh
wattsim replay session.jsonl --export session.ws
```

The scenario sends the charger's recorded messages and nothing else: `boot`, `heartbeat` and `authorize` where those send exactly the recorded message, `send ACTION {payload}` for the rest. The CSMS's answers become `expect result` and `expect error`, its CALLs become `expect call`, and the charger's answers to them become `on` handlers. `wait` statements keep the recorded gaps. The output passes `wattsim check` and `wattsim fmt --check`.

From there it is an ordinary scenario: run it with `wattsim run`, loosen the assertions you do not care about, multiply the station into a fleet, or add chaos rules.

## 5. Draft a quirk profile

If the recording shows the charger bending the spec, `wattsim quirk derive session.jsonl -o draft.ws` turns those findings into a [quirk profile](/docs/guides/quirks) that any simulated station can apply.

## Trace format

Recordings are [Open OCPP Trace](https://github.com/open-ocpp-trace/specification) v1.1 by default, one JSON record per message. `--format frames` writes wattsim's own line format instead, which also keeps frames that format has no record for. The [trace reference](/docs/reference/trace) has the details.
